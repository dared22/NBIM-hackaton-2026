To Our Shareholders,
2025 was a year of deliberate focus and execution at Biohaven. We advanced programs with strong scientific foundations, sharpened our strategic priorities, and reinforced financial discipline—while remaining deeply committed to our mission of delivering transformative therapies for patients. Our progress in 2025 and into the new year strengthens our confidence in Biohaven’s strategy and our ability to create durable long-term shareholder value.

Revolutionizing the Inflammation and Immunology Treatment Paradigm Through Extracellular Protein Degradation

Our most significant advances in 2025 came from our MoDE and TRAP extracellular protein degrader platform, which remains the central pillar of our long-term strategy in inflammation and immunology. These technologies enable the selective, rapid removal of disease-causing circulating proteins while preserving healthy immune function—representing a fundamentally new approach to treating immune-mediated disease.

Key achievements include:

•IgA Nephropathy (BHV-1400): First dosing in IgAN patients resulted in early observations of both biomarker and clinical responses, including selective lowering of disease-causing Gd-IgA1, improvements in proteinuria, hematuria, fatigue, and kidney function. These results build on strong Phase 1 data demonstrating rapid and sustained reductions in Gd-IgA1 exceeding 80% after a single subcutaneous dose. A pivotal study is expected to initiate in early 2026.
•Graves’ Disease (BHV-1300): First-in-patient clinical experience demonstrated complete suppression of pathogenic TSH receptor–stimulating antibodies with normalization of thyroid hormone levels within weeks. Together with best-in-class IgG reductions observed in healthy volunteers, these data support our plan to initiate a pivotal study in 2026.

With this program, we are entering a new era of precision immunology, and Biohaven has the most advanced technologies in clinical testing to lead the way. There is no other immunologic-modulating platform that targets disease through extracellular protein degradation in the way that our MoDE and TRAP degraders do. We are the first in the clinic to harness the body’s natural clearance mechanisms to selectively remove disease-causing proteins—effectively plucking out the pathogenic drivers of disease while leaving the rest of the immune system intact. For patients, this approach has the potential to fundamentally change their treatment outlook: faster results, fewer tradeoffs, and therapies that can be administered simply and safely at home. Just as we helped define a new treatment paradigm with Nurtec by targeting disease biology with precision and scalability, we believe extracellular protein degradation has the potential to create a similarly transformative opportunity across multiple large immune-mediated diseases.

Importantly, the market opportunity for extracellular protein degradation is significant. As we think about what Biohaven achieved with Nurtec—creating a new category, expanding access, and reaching millions of patients globally—we see a similar opportunity for our degrader platform to transform treatment standards across multiple indications over time. While still early, we believe this platform has the potential to be a foundational, multi-billion-dollar franchise that drives long-term growth and impact.

Advancing a Differentiated Epilepsy Franchise

Our selective Kv7 ion channel activator, opakalim, continues to demonstrate the potential to be a paradigm-shifting therapy for focal epilepsy.

Highlights include:

•In an ongoing open-label extension study, the majority of participants treated for at least six months achieved ≥50% reductions in seizure frequency compared to pretreatment baseline.
•Opakalim was well tolerated, with a low incidence of central nervous system adverse events, representing a highly differentiated safety profile relative to other antiseizure medicines.

We believe opakalim addresses a significant unmet need for effective seizure control without the burden of limiting side effects, and we expect to deliver pivotal results in 2026.







Targeting High-Quality Weight Loss in Obesity

Our third key program, taldefgrobep alfa, reflects our commitment to pursuing differentiated biology in large, underserved markets.

Key developments include:

•Initiation of a Phase 2 proof-of-concept study in obesity in the fourth quarter of 2025, with topline results expected in 2026.
•A unique mechanism designed to reduce fat mass while increasing lean muscle and bone density, supported by favorable Phase 1 body composition data and a strong safety record across more than 700 participants.

We believe taldefgrobep has the potential to redefine what constitutes meaningful and sustainable weight loss and to address important limitations of existing therapies.

Demonstrating Platform Breadth in Oncology

In oncology, recent clinical results further demonstrate the breadth and versatility of Biohaven’s science beyond our core immunology focus. Our Trop2 ADC is demonstrating a highly differentiated profile in regard to safety and efficacy in initial clinical testing, and our FGFR3 targeting ADC is the first of its kind in clinical testing.

Key updates include:

•BHV-1510 (Trop2 ADC): Continued clinical activity observed in non-small cell lung, endometrial, and urothelial cancers, including confirmed responses in heavily pretreated patients, with a differentiated and manageable safety profile. Compelling preliminary efficacy has been shown at the 2.5mg/kg Q3W dosing in combination with anti-PD(L)1 with confirmed overall response rate of 73% (8/11). Notably, BHV-1510 is the first and only Trop2 ADC in the clinic with the potential for subcutaneous administration, reflecting a design philosophy consistent with our patient-centric approach.
•BHV-1530 (FGFR3 ADC): Ongoing dose escalation continues without dose-limiting toxicities to date, supporting further development in FGFR3-driven malignancies and we are now exploring doses in the expected therapeutic range.

These encouraging results reinforce the depth of Biohaven’s discovery and translational capabilities. Given the strength of the data and the capital requirements of oncology development, we will continue evaluating strategic partnership opportunities and other creative pathways to best advance these programs while maximizing value for shareholders.

Sustaining Innovation Through Discovery

Our discovery engine continues to generate the next wave of innovation, advancing multiple novel programs with the potential for future clinical development or strategic partnering, including:

•BHV-1955 for tinnitus targeting the oxytocin receptor,
•BHV-2120, a brain-penetrant TRPM3 inhibitor for epilepsy,
•BHV-8555, targeting alpha-synuclein aggregation in Parkinson’s disease, and
•BHV-8100, a PKM2 activator for neurodegenerative disorders and aging.

These programs reflect our commitment to sustained innovation while maintaining a disciplined and intentional cadence of advancement.

Financial Discipline and Strategic Focus

In parallel with pipeline progress, we redoubled our commitment to financial discipline:

•Aligned spending with clear development milestones while preserving flexibility for future opportunities.
•Strengthened our balance sheet through disciplined capital raising and strategic financing initiatives.

This approach positions Biohaven to efficiently execute through upcoming milestones while maintaining optionality across our broader portfolio.

We are working toward a future in which disease is precisely targeted, treatments are simpler and safer, and meaningful impact can be delivered at scale. Days matter for patients waiting—and the year ahead promises to be one of continued progress.

If you are a beneficial owner and do not instruct your broker, bank, or other agent how to vote your shares, the question of whether your broker or nominee will still be able to vote your shares depends on whether the NYSE deems the particular proposal to be a “routine” matter. Brokers and nominees can use their discretion to vote “uninstructed” shares with respect to matters that are considered to be “routine,” but not with respect to “non-routine” matters. Under the rules and interpretations of the NYSE, “non-routine” matters are matters that may substantially affect the rights or privileges of shareholders, such as mergers, shareholder proposals, elections of directors (even if not contested), executive compensation (including any advisory shareholder votes on executive compensation, or advisory shareholder votes on the frequency of shareholder votes on executive compensation), and certain corporate governance proposals, even if management-supported. EXECUTIVE COMPENSATION PROGRAM
In line with our guiding principles described above, the objectives of our compensation program are to attract, retain and motivate our executive officers to meet our long-term goals, and to align the interests of our executive officers with those of the Company's shareholders. To achieve our goals, we provide appropriate levels of risk and reward, assessed on a relative basis to our peers at the executive levels within the Company and in proportion to individual contribution and performance. We establish appropriate incentives for management to further the Company’s long-term strategic goals and avoid emphasis on increase in short-term market value.
The compensation committee of our Board (the “compensation committee”) reviews compensation annually for all employees, including our executives. In determining recommendations to make to the independent members of the Board regarding the setting of executive base salaries, annual bonuses and bonus opportunities, and granting equity awards for our executive officers, the compensation committee considers compensation for comparable positions in the market, the historical compensation levels of our executives, individual performance as compared to our expectations and objectives, and our desire to motivate our employees to achieve short- and long-term results that are in the best interests of our shareholders and to promote a long-term commitment to the Company. We consider the executive compensation of our peers in setting our executive pay levels and make adjustments to account for personal experience, performance and internal equity. In making our annual equity awards, grants are sized after evaluating and considering the Company’s performance over the fiscal year, each executive’s individual contributions to our accomplishments over the year (based on the executive’s and the CEO’s assessment of their performance) and the size of the award each executive received in the prior fiscal year.
Compensation Committee Role and Process
The compensation committee makes most of the significant adjustments to annual compensation, determine bonus and equity awards and establish new performance objectives at one or more meetings held during the first quarter of the year. The compensation committee will also consider matters related to individual compensation, such as compensation for new executive hires, as well as high-level strategic issues, such as the efficacy of our compensation strategy, potential modifications to that strategy and new trends, plans or approaches to compensation, at various meetings throughout the year.
Independent members of our Board determine our executives’ compensation after receiving recommendations from our compensation committee. The compensation committee consists of three members of the Board, and each member of the compensation committee is independent (as determined under the independence requirements of the New York Stock Exchange). The compensation committee’s executive compensation duties and policies include the following:
▪Reviewing and approving corporate goals and objectives that reinforce the Company’s long-term strategic goals
▪Approving, and recommending to the independent members of the Board for approval, levels of executive compensation
▪Establishing policies with respect to equity compensation arrangements, including compensation plans and programs advisable for the Company
▪Reviewing regional and industry-wide compensation practices and trends to assess the adequacy and competitiveness of the Company’s compensation practices
▪Evaluating the efficacy of the Company’s compensation policy and strategy in achieving expected benefits to the Company and otherwise furthering the compensation committee’s policy
▪Implementing and administering the Company's clawback policy
▪Reviewing and approving the terms of any NEO employment arrangements, severance arrangements, change in control protections and any other compensatory arrangements
Each year, our compensation committee typically reviews and discusses management's proposed compensation for all executives. The CEO meets with the compensation committee to review the compensation levels for the Company’s executive-level officers (other than our CEO) as well as the overall goals for the Company and individuals. The compensation committee solicits and considers evaluations and recommendations submitted to the compensation committee by the Chief Executive Officer with respect to individual employee performance. Based on those discussions and after receiving recommendations from the CEO and the compensation committee, our Board, in its discretion and without members of management present, discusses and ultimately approves the compensation of our executive officers.

Long-Term Incentives
Our equity-based incentive awards are designed to align our executives’ interests with those of our shareholders. Our compensation committee is responsible for reviewing and recommending equity grants to the independent members of the Board for their approval. Vesting of equity awards is generally tied to continuous service with the Company over the vesting schedule and serves as an additional retention measure. Additional grants may occur periodically in order to specifically incentivize executives with respect to achieving certain corporate goals or to reward executives for exceptional performance.
All of the Company's currently outstanding equity award grants to executives have been made in the form of stock options and RSUs. Stock options and RSUs are designed to align the interests of the Company’s executives with those of shareholders by encouraging executives to enhance the value of the Company and, hence, the price of our common shares. Our stock options are granted with a per share exercise price equal to no less than the fair market value of a common share on the date of the grant. The structure of the equity program recommended by the compensation committee to the independent members of the Board (including the mix of equity awards and the relative weight assigned to each type of award) is reviewed annually by the compensation committee and its independent compensation consultant, and aims to motivate sustainable stock price appreciation, promote executive retention, limit unnecessary risk-taking and minimize shareholder dilution, while remaining competitive with market practices for similarly situated companies.

RELATED-PERSON TRANSACTIONS POLICY AND PROCEDURES
We have adopted a written Related Person Transaction Policy that sets forth our procedures for the identification, review, consideration and approval or ratification of related person transactions. For purposes of our policy only, a related person transaction is a transaction, arrangement or relationship, or any series of similar transactions, arrangements or relationships, in which we and any related person are, were or will be participants in which the amount involved exceeds $120,000. Transactions involving compensation for services provided to us as an employee or director are not covered by this policy. A related person is any executive officer, director or beneficial owner of more than 5% of any class of our voting securities, including any of their immediate family members and any entity owned or controlled by such persons.
Under the policy, if a transaction has been identified as a related person transaction, including any transaction that was not a related person transaction when originally consummated or any transaction that was not initially identified as a related person transaction prior to consummation, our management must present information regarding the related person transaction to our audit committee, or, if audit committee approval would be inappropriate, to another independent body of our Board, for review, consideration and approval or ratification. The presentation must include a description of, among other things, the material facts, the interests, direct and indirect, of the related persons, the benefits to us of the transaction and whether the transaction is on terms that are comparable to the terms available to or from, as the case may be, an unrelated third party or to or from employees generally. Under the policy, we will collect information that we deem reasonably necessary from each director, executive officer and, to the extent feasible, significant shareholder to enable us to identify any existing or potential related person transactions and to effectuate the terms of the policy. In addition, under our Code of Business Conduct and Ethics, our employees and directors have an affirmative responsibility to disclose any transaction or relationship that reasonably could be expected to give rise to a conflict of interest. In considering related person transactions, our audit committee, or other independent body of our Board, will take into account the relevant available facts and circumstances including, but not limited to:
•    the risks, costs and benefits to us;
•    the impact on a director’s independence in the event that the related person is a director, immediate family member of a director or an entity with which a director is affiliated;
•    the availability of other sources for comparable services or products; and
•    the terms available to or from, as the case may be, unrelated third parties or to or from employees generally.
The policy requires that, in determining whether to approve, ratify or reject a related person transaction, our audit committee, or other independent body of our Board, must consider, in light of known circumstances, whether the transaction is in, or is not inconsistent with, our best interests and those of our shareholders, as our audit committee, or other independent body of our Board, determines in the good faith exercise of its discretion.
